Adam Abdulkadir — Behavioral Interview Guide

Core framework
The natural STAR formula. Don't think Situation → Task → Action → Result. Think:

1. What was happening?
2. What was the problem?
3. What did I actually do?
4. What happened after?
Good answers sound like
  • Reflective, not rehearsed
  • Include tradeoffs
  • Explain your thinking
  • Engineer talking naturally
  • Structured without sounding it
Bad answers sound like
  • Memorized scripts
  • Over-polished
  • Buzzword soup
  • Too much detail
  • No clear outcome
Delivery rules

Slow down

Speak 15–20% slower than feels natural. Pause more. Simplify explanations. Avoid over-explaining.

You naturally think quickly and deeply — in interviews that can come across as rapid-fire technical dumping. Calm and measured always reads stronger than fast and thorough.

Don't try to sound senior

You already sound stronger than your years of experience because you think systemically, understand production systems, understand operational risk, and understand architecture.

Your only goal is: communicate clearly and calmly. That alone separates you from most candidates at your level.

Be conversational, not corporate

No fake enthusiasm. No buzzword soup. No over-rehearsed answers.

Aim for: conversational · confident · grounded · slightly technical · concise · adaptable.

Situational & behavioral

Conflict with a coworker

Say this
I haven't had major interpersonal conflict, but I've had situations where engineers disagreed on implementation approaches or priorities. I try to focus discussions around tradeoffs — maintainability, operational risk, delivery timelines, long-term scalability — rather than who's right. Keeping it technical and collaborative usually resolves things quickly.

Say this

Explain a technical concept to a non-technical person

Pick a concept from your work — like what a microservice is, or what an NPE means — and explain it plainly without jargon. Demonstrate awareness of your audience. The point isn't the answer, it's showing you can adjust your communication register.

Calm > fast
Slow answers that show thinking always outperform fast answers that show nerves. If you need a second to think, take it. "Let me think about that for a second" is a green flag, not a red one.
Tradeoffs > certainty
The best engineers talk about tradeoffs, not perfect solutions. "We chose X over Y because of Z" shows more seniority than "X was the right answer."
Story > resume
Your resume lists what you did. Your stories explain how you think. The interview is about the thinking, not the bullet points.
The final rule: Communicate clearly and calmly. That alone separates you from most candidates at your level.
